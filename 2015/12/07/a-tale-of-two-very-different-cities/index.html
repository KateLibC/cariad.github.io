A tale of two very different cities

Dec 7, 2015

Edinburgh skyline as seen from next to Waverly Station

Last year, I had finally visited Scotland–Edinburgh to be exact. It was a lovely city with an excellent public transport system to the point where Waverly Station–located in a former lake around the original city centre–became one of my favourite railway stations in the world. The buses and the single tram line made it quite easy for myself to navigate and visit friends during one of the busiest times of year: the Fringe.

The less glamourous city of Chiliwack as viewed at something I want to complain about

In the same year a month prior, I attended a wedding in Chilliwack, British Columbia. You might find it a bit jarring for me to go from a medieval city with haphazard roads barely capable of permitting private automobiles to a rural municipality sort of nestled in the Cascade Mountains that has its foundings seven centuries after and is completely ruled by the automobile. The comparisons I can make between the two cities would on the surface be impossible right?

The simple answer of course is that both have had public transportation at one point destroyed in favour of the automobile and anyone would be justified in saying that there’s nothing more. I would agree with that, but there is something Chilliwack has that felt jarring to me and to find myself in Edinburgh a few weeks after said wedding made this stupid blog post idea linger in my brain for the past year and a half so let’s just go down this road shall we?
